ECE · Communications Networks 2025
Online Gaming Traffic Performance over WAN Bottleneck
Queueing Theory · M/M/1/B · Mininet · UDP/TCP Contention · QoE
  • Modeled a hierarchical access–WAN–server topology (20 Mbps bottleneck, 50-packet buffer, 92ms propagation RTT) using M/M/1/B queueing theory; derived service rate μ=19,531 pkt/s, gaming arrival rate λ_g=976.6 pkt/s; showed analytically that jitter grows as √(ρ/μ²(1−ρ)²) and packet loss as P_loss=(1−ρ)ρᴮ/(1−ρᴮ⁺¹), predicting rapid jitter increase before mean RTT changes.
  • Emulated two scenarios in Mininet: (A) 0/2/3 long-lived TCP background flows — jitter increased from 0.038ms (baseline) to 0.353ms at 2 flows, packet loss peaked at 0.25%; non-monotonic loss at 3 flows (0.055%) explained by TCP burst timing relative to gaming packet arrivals at buffer overflow events. (B) 100 short-burst UDP sessions (50KB, ~0.39s each, 0.1s gap) — typical jitter 0.02–0.08ms and 0% loss, but worst-case isolated bursts hit 17% packet loss and 1.7ms jitter from transient buffer overflow.
  • Confirmed that mean RTT (~92.5ms) remained stable across all contention scenarios, validating that RTT alone is insufficient as a QoE metric for gaming; jitter and packet loss under transient congestion are the dominant performance determinants for UDP-based real-time traffic.
